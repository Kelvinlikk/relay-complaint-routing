Who should read this complaint?

“My payment never posted.” A card team and a mortgage team could both receive that message. Relay uses the complaint and information known at intake to recommend a specialist. When the evidence is weak, a reviewer chooses the destination.

Why each desk needs its own rule

One confidence cutoff appeared to meet a 5% error target overall in July. But among its 106 credit-report recommendations, 37 disagreed with the recorded product. The average hid a problem that would matter to the person receiving the complaint.

What about the cases people keep?

Under a later proposal to hold short messages, reviewers would have kept 13,615 cases in an expanded May replay. For those cases, Relay’s two suggestions included the recorded product 91.9% of the time, compared with 63.6% for a keyword rule. Whether a shortlist actually helps reviewers work faster or more accurately is a separate question.

The pilot decision

Start with staff confirming every handoff. Independently audit 400 recommendations per candidate desk, then separately randomize whether reviewers see two suggestions. Measure active review time, routing accuracy and serious delays before considering automation.
