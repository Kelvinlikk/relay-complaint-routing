# Research brief: what the historical test is actually testing

**The credit-reporting problem did not disappear; it became nearly invisible in the public text.** We audited all raw archive rows in the two original test windows, before sampling or deduplication. In June 2026, credit reporting accounted for **91.1% of the 620,794 records** but only **4.44% of the 16,789 records containing at least ten words of public narrative**. This is selection into the study itself. A later dated holdout can be honest without being representative of an actual bank inbox.

| Period / product | All archive records | ≥10 words available | Eligible / all |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sep 2020–Apr 2021: credit reporting | 211,814 | 65,016 | 30.69% |
| Sep 2020–Apr 2021: other products | 118,525 | 58,353 | 49.23% |
| June 2026: credit reporting | 565,831 | 745 | 0.13% |
| June 2026: other products | 54,963 | 16,044 | 29.19% |

June credit reports had a public narrative of any length in 751 rows; excluding short narratives reduced that to 745. Repeated-template screening cannot explain this raw discrepancy. The independent audit discloses counts only: no narratives, identifiers, or output probabilities. Raw populations in this table are published archive records, not the total population of consumers or complaints received by individual banks. The study's retained June sample was 140/3,621 (3.87%) credit reporting after additional screening.

**What a change in the product mix can explain.** Holding the old router fixed, reweight the original test rows so the true product proportions agree with the June sample. Reweight the incorrect and automatic counts separately, then divide. This estimates what *would have happened if the old within-product routing behavior had remained constant*. It is a diagnostic using retrospective product labels, not an operational targeting rule.

| Fixed original routing policy | Coverage | Wrong / all automatic routes | Wrong / routes to credit reports |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Historical test, observed | 54.46% | 4.01% | 2.01% |
| Historical within-product behavior, June true-product mix | 34.43% | 5.84% | 28.71% |
| June test, observed | 31.23% | 12.02% | 46.15% |

Mix alone would have breached the 5% target under this replay, but does not reproduce the June outcome. The remaining gap is consistent with changes within products and sampling variation. Do not attribute it to particular portal policies, fraud, model architecture or consumer behavior without further evidence.

**Contemporaneous operational context.** The CFPB's [June 24, 2026 release](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/the-cfpb-is-correcting-flaws-to-restore-integrity-and-utility-to-the-consumer-complaint-system/) says it had added a notice encouraging consumers with credit-report inaccuracies to dispute directly with the reporting agency before contacting the Bureau. It also describes identity verification and clearer third-party disclosures. The release does not give an exact intervention date for each change. It separately discusses proposed changes; do not present a proposal as deployed. [June/July API release notes](https://cfpb.github.io/api/ccdb/release-notes.html) describe export field and format changes. Neither source establishes a cause of the routing failure. On [August 14](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/the-cfpb-to-cease-discretionary-publication-of-complaint-narratives-and-visualizations/), the Bureau announced it would cease publishing narratives; earlier records remain in its [FOIA narratives archive](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/foia-requests/foia-electronic-reading-room/cfpb-consumer-complaint-database-narratives-archive/). This is why raw monthly archives rather than the live API are necessary for reproducibility.

**What the labels mean.** The [CFPB data dictionary](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/data-use/) defines product as what the consumer identified, company as the business complained about, state as the mailing state. These labels are proxies for the correct internal work queue; no independent worker adjudicated the archived labels. Accept a tenant identifier only when independently recorded by the client mailbox before routing. The [database overview](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/) says public complaints are not a statistical sample of consumer experiences. Complaint receipt dates define a chronological archive replay, not proof that all public labels and text were knowable on those dates. A live pilot requires timestamped adjudications and handling outcomes.

**Business case: $30/hour is plausible, not verified at a client.** The [BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/customer-service-representatives.htm) reports a national median wage of $21.53/hour for customer-service representatives in May 2025. Its [June 2026 compensation survey](https://www.bls.gov/charts/employer-costs-for-employee-compensation/costs-per-hour.htm) reports $32.82 in wages out of $46.89/hour total compensation across private-industry workers, or 70.0%. Using the broad burden share as a rough proxy yields $21.53/0.70 = $30.76/hour. These are different populations and survey periods; this is a reasonableness check, not an estimate of bank intake labor. The four-minute first pass and $25 misroute cost remain scenario inputs. With $30/hour, four minutes saves $2; $2/$25 = 8% is the pre-overhead economic break-even misroute rate, separate from the 5% quality ceiling. Recovered staff capacity is not cash savings unless schedules or output actually change.

**From literature to operating controls.** [Geifman & El-Yaniv (NeurIPS 2017)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html) motivates reporting both coverage and risk conditional on acceptance. [Lipton, Wang & Smola (ICML 2018)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/lipton18a.html) explicitly assumes within-label input distributions are stable under pure label shift. Our descriptive test above illustrates why a prior-only fix cannot be presumed sufficient. [Angelopoulos et al. (2025)](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/learn-then-test-calibrating-predictive-algorithms-achieve-risk) explains why screening multiple candidate policies complicates risk claims. Choice of thresholds on one period and evaluation on a separate period is appropriate; post-selection bounds are not blanket future guarantees. Use independent labels, destination-level evidence and a fallback to a human reviewer whenever the evidence is sparse or stale.

**Course application.** Project 1 asks “What problem? What can AI do? Does it work? Does it matter?” The customer-facing decision is one product assignment or a human handoff. Text transfer learning (Lecture 13's model-adaptation principle) and contextual representations (Lecture 14) should earn their place in a direct comparison; poor incremental results are a finding, not an embarrassment. Keeping near-duplicates out of later periods applies Lecture 14's split-before-augmentation lesson to templates rather than images. Following the economics of HW7, two equally probable model errors can have different practical consequences. Report per-queue risk, the opportunity cost of abstaining and sensitivity to staffing/rework assumptions. Do not force an image branch or describe a deep model as the production winner unless measured.
