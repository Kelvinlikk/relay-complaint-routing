# Where 15.071 changes this project
Course sources: Alexandre Jacquillat, *15.071 The AI Edge*, Fall 2026, lecture PDFs 1–14 and the team project brief (pp. 1–3). Slide numbers below are the printed lecture numbers. Reports of what Relay actually did are backed by source or aggregate files.

| Lecture and pages | Teaching point applied | Where to verify / what we did not claim |
|---|---|---|
| 1, pp. 35, 42 | Evaluate outputs against their intended business action; communicate to a decision-maker. | [Decision record](decision.md), synthetic review desk, tested release/hold cases. No generative agent: this is Predictive AI, not Project 2. |
| 2, pp. 12, 33–39 | Evaluate predictions on later observations; turn an operational decision into money. | Chronological train/tune/policy/test; [scenario model](../experiments/research/pilot_economics.py). No attempt to forecast electricity or predict a continuous complaint value. |
| 3, pp. 20–33 | Logistic classification, class errors and the business meaning of a cutoff. | [Original model](../src/fit_classic.py), confusion tables and [destination-specific cutoffs](../experiments/policy/README-policy.md). Misrouting among actions is the decision metric; macro-F1 summarizes all labels. |
| 4, pp. 15, 23–29, 32 | Regularization and separate validation for model choice. | [Tuning evidence](../results/linear_tuning.json); tuning, policy choice and later evaluation are separated. Chronological holdouts replace random folds because deployment happens later. |
| 5, pp. 11–12, 33–37 | Simple/interpretable baselines and break-even value. | Handwritten [keyword rule](../experiments/transfer/keywords.json), manual review, cost sensitivity. Used the decision lesson rather than fitting a single CART for its own sake. |
| 6, pp. 9, 26–33, 37 | Tree ensembles, temporal validation and interpretation through controlled comparisons. | CatBoost on client and state, blended with sparse text; [matched ablations](../experiments/deep/README.md). Known client is an input requirement, not a bank identity inferred after referral. |
| 7, pp. 27–29, 34–36 | Compare a ranked recommendation with popularity; evaluate hit-at-k for the action the user takes. | Two desk suggestions vs two fixed June products and two keyword suggestions on the **same held records**. There are no user–item ratings: no collaborative filtering claim. |
| 8, pp. 23–27, 33–36 | Learned representations and how practical constraints change the use of probabilities. | Seven-way neural classifier and joint representation head; choose a model for each task with results, not parameter count. |
| 9, pp. 27–34, 41–45 | Mini-batch training and selecting a checkpoint on validation, not on test results. | [Training code](../src/fit_text.py) and four recorded validation epochs. The best checkpoint was kept; the run did not terminate early. |
| 10, pp. 20–29, 32–38 | Compare sparse word features with semantic representations. | Fixed TF-IDF and a fine-tuned contextual encoder; controlled feature comparison across historical, June and July intervals. No GloVe result is claimed. |
| 11, pp. 36–39 | Ticket routing; confidence versus coverage; compare errors across destinations. | Direct analogue, extended to real CFPB narratives, leakage controls, a broken old threshold, and a single cutoff masking a 34.9% error for one desk. |
| 12, pp. 22–26, 33–39 | Transfer learning plus the distinction between automation and augmenting staff. | Fine-tuned TinyBERT challenger. Auto-handoffs and ranked assistance now have separate denominators, costs and outcome tests. A shortlist hit is not staff accuracy. |
| 13, pp. 82–83 | A pilot measures its input environment as well as its model. | Raw text-availability audit; precommitted August evaluation was unusable; local shadow pilot. No visual inspection or CNN for text-only inputs. |
| 14, pp. 36–38 | Evaluate retrieval at more than one rank. | Top-one vs top-two over product labels; no raw images, image patches, ViT or image similarity. |
