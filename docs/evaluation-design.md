# Evaluation protocol

> **Historical phase.** This document records the original policy and its 2026 stress test. For the extended June audit, frozen July evaluation and current pilot decision, read [the decision record](decision.md).
**Unit:** one intake decision for one complaint. Six routes are in scope: Accounts, Cards (including prepaid), Collections, Credit reports, Mortgages and Transfers. Everything else is pooled as “Other / review,” a seventh classifier label that can never auto-route. “Correct” means agreement with the original selected product after this mapping; a specialist did not adjudicate these labels.

**Sampling and timing:** Filter for published text of at least ten whitespace-separated words. Sample by seeded BLAKE2b hash of complaint ID within five periods, before deduplication, without rebalancing products. Train May 2018–Dec 2019; model tuning Jan–Apr 2020; policy cutoff May–Aug 2020; primary test Sep 2020–Apr 2021; external stress test June 2026. Neither later set was used to choose this historical model or cutoff. June 2026 was subsequently reused to develop the separate destination rules; July was the temporal test for those rules. Source counts, period counts and archive hashes are in results/cohort.json. Published records, rather than the entire intake stream, are the evaluation population; very short and unpublished complaints were not evaluated.

**Template leakage:** Retain the earliest canonical text (case, redaction masks, punctuation, numbers and whitespace normalized). Screen later candidates using 64-permutation MinHash LSH on word trigrams; discard a candidate at Jaccard similarity of at least 0.80 to an earlier retained text. This approximate search can miss paraphrases and some near-duplicates. It also suppresses repeated templates, so this measures generalization to new language rather than the savings from repeated forms.

**Inputs:** Free text alone; or company/tenant and state alone; or their combination. No product hierarchy, issue, submission outcome, response, timeliness or complaint ID enters the predictors. The company recorded by CFPB is the recipient, which may only be used as a surrogate for a tenant independently known at intake. State is optional. The public package includes no consumer profiles, postal codes or case-level records.

**Models:** Majority class; TF-IDF word unigrams and bigrams with L2 logistic regression, tuned C; CatBoost with categorical company and state; a fully fine-tuned pretrained BERT-Tiny (two layers, 128 hidden units, 112 positions: up to 110 WordPiece tokens plus two special tokens; for longer messages keep the first 78 and last 32 tokens, four epochs, best tuning macro-F1); a calibrated linear probability mixture with weights on a 0.2 grid, minimizing tuning log loss. Model-specific temperatures minimize tuning log loss, then the selected blend has its own temperature. Tuning metrics are descriptive; only later windows support outcome claims.

**Policy:** For each model, use a fixed 0.50–0.995 cutoff grid in 0.005 increments. On the policy window choose the greatest coverage with at least 100 automatic cases and a two-sided 95% Wilson upper bound on error no greater than 5%; if none qualifies, refer all. Choose the model with the greatest eligible policy coverage. Ties favor the earlier candidate in code. This is a selection heuristic, not a distribution-free guarantee: many cutoffs are tried. Score the single locked policy on both later periods. “Other” predictions always refer; mistaken auto-routes of true Other complaints count as errors.

**Uncertainty:** Report two-sided Wilson intervals on misroutes / auto-routes and auto-routes / eligible arrivals. Binomial intervals are descriptive and assume independent cases. A supplementary bootstrap resamples eight test months as clusters (1,000 draws). This does not estimate generalization to a new organization or macroeconomic regime. Macro-F1 is the unweighted average across all seven labels. Per-class recall is pre-abstention; per-destination routing risk and coverage are separate measures.

**Impact:** Relative to reviewing every arrival: avoided first-pass reviews times minutes/60 times loaded hourly labor cost, minus the number of wrong automatic routes times the incremental rework cost. An incorrect automatic route receives a rework charge; the avoided first pass is counted once. This historical scenario excludes fixed integration costs, quality-assurance sampling and downstream costs beyond the assumptions. The [current July sensitivity table](../results/pilot_economics.csv) separately models spot audits. No staff timing study or realized operating savings are claimed.
