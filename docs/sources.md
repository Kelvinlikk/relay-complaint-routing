# Research notes and source trail
1. [CFPB Consumer Complaint Database Narratives Archive](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/foia-requests/foia-electronic-reading-room/cfpb-consumer-complaint-database-narratives-archive/). Sources of the two original ZIPs; the [next-month evaluation](../experiments/external/README.md) describes the third. Historic file: May 2018–April 2021; stress file: June 2026. The source links and SHA-256 hashes are in the code and results/cohort.json. Sampling does not select labels or favor large institutions.
2. [CFPB database and product updates](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/). Products reflect the choices on the form at the time. Published complaints are not representative of the market. Only complaints forwarded to companies for response are eligible. The classifier predicts this recorded category, not who is responsible or whether a complaint is justified. New labels are mapped into the named destinations or the manual-review bucket.
3. [CFPB field definitions](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/data-use/). “Company” is the company the CFPB sent the complaint to. It would leak routing information if populated after intake. The proposed user is a service provider serving several pre-identified clients: accept this field only if independently established from the incoming mailbox. The original experiment is an optimistic proxy, not proof that production has this field. The current desk shows a text-only suggestion to a reviewer if the client is not independently known; it does not enable an automatic transfer. A single-bank use case needs separate local evaluation.
4. [CFPB release notes](https://cfpb.github.io/api/ccdb/release-notes.html) and [agency announcement](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/the-cfpb-to-cease-discretionary-publication-of-complaint-narratives-and-visualizations/). Historical narrative archives, not the live export, make this experiment reproducible. Historical disclosure is selective; results on this public sample do not establish representativeness or equal service.
5. [BERT-Tiny model card](https://huggingface.co/prajjwal1/bert-tiny), [Turc et al., small pretrained language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08962). We fine-tune both layers and the classifier, rather than using fixed embeddings. Model revision pinned in fit_text.py. Smaller encoders make an inexpensive benchmark, not necessarily the most accurate.
6. [Guo et al., On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a). Fit a single temperature on held-out tuning data to calibrate score magnitudes; it cannot fix dataset shift. [Geifman & El-Yaniv, SelectiveNet](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/geifman19a.html) motivates measuring risk conditional on acceptance and coverage. We implement a confidence cutoff, not the SelectiveNet architecture.
7. Distinct application precedent: [CFPB bank complaints decision benchmark](https://huggingface.co/datasets/hvgupta1/cfpb-bank-complaints-benchmark). One-bank demonstration uses three product labels. Our independent experiment uses raw official archives, multiple providers, six specialist destinations plus review, a later policy window, and an external year. We do not use that benchmark's training set or results.

For the extension, the [official CFPB archive](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/foia-requests/foia-electronic-reading-room/cfpb-consumer-complaint-database-narratives-archive/) also supplied the [May snapshot](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/CCDB_Export_18_May_2026.zip) and [August snapshot](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/CCDB_Export_21_August_2026.zip). The [August 14 announcement](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/the-cfpb-to-cease-discretionary-publication-of-complaint-narratives-and-visualizations/) provides publication context, not a causal explanation for the observed product mix. Archive hashes, eligibility counts and registered protocols are in `experiments/transfer/`.
