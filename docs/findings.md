# Complaint routing: evidence for a shadow pilot

> **Historical phase.** This document records the original policy and its 2026 stress test. For the extended June audit, frozen July evaluation and current pilot decision, read [the decision record](decision.md).
**Decision:** Do not authorize unconditional automation. Run a shadow pilot in a multi-client operations inbox and validate each destination locally. The model predicts the recorded product, not the correct resolution or whether a consumer’s allegation is valid.
## What we measured
40,382 dated narratives; six specialist destinations plus a manual-only other bucket. Train, tuning, policy validation and two tests are separate; duplicate screening discarded 4,618 sampled records. Later sets were unseen during fitting and operating-rule selection. The seven-class model comparison is in results/benchmarks.csv; class-specific recall is in results/queue_metrics.csv.
The selected Combined rule at 0.900 sent **3,767/6,917** primary-test cases directly (54.5%, 95% CI 53.3–55.6%); **151** were wrong, or 4.0% of automatic routes (95% CI 3.4–4.7%). Reusing it in June 2026 sent **1,131/3,621** directly (31.2%, 95% CI 29.7–32.8%); **136** were wrong (12.0%, 95% CI 10.3–14.1%). Coverage, interval estimates and all five model policies are in results/policy.json.
## Why the later test matters
Credit-report complaints fell from 48.2% to 3.9% of these evaluated samples. This is a shift in the archive, not evidence about complaint incidence in the market. The weight given to company, changes in vocabulary and changes in product options can all matter; the comparison does not establish which caused the change. The largest wrong-route flow in June was Collections → Credit reports (53). The hypothesis is that related narratives mention the same products in different roles; confirm it by reviewing live cases.
The best blend gave zero weight to BERT: 80% TF-IDF and 20% metadata. A separately tuned BERT-plus-metadata model reached macro-F1 0.754 on the primary test and 0.688 in June; the selected blend reached 0.775 and 0.694. There is no reason to pay the transformer inference cost for the winning rule. In June, 108 of the 136 errors went to credit reports (108/234 sent there); only 4 of 221 routes to cards were wrong (upper 95% bound 4.6%). Explore cards first in shadow mode, not a post-hoc guarantee. When the tenant was masked but the combined policy left unchanged it made 105 errors among 600 automatic June routes (17.5%); a separately calibrated text-only policy is a distinct fallback and must also be validated locally.
## Economics
At four minutes per first-pass review, $30/hour and $25 in additional handling per wrong route, net value per 10,000 eligible arrivals is **$5,434** on the primary test and **-$3,143** on the 2026 test. At those costs the break-even error rate is 8% among automatic routes ($2/$25). The 5% quality target is stricter than economic break-even. Fixed costs, spot audits, downstream consumer harm and implementation costs are excluded. No operational savings or staff time study was observed.
## Operational decision
A known tenant must be supplied by the inbound channel; CFPB’s eventual recipient must never be backfilled at serving time. In the historical baseline, a missing client triggered a text-only cutoff. The [current rule](decision.md) supersedes it: a missing verified client always keeps the case with staff. A text-only score may suggest a desk; it cannot authorize a handoff. Do not automate high-priority escalations merely because the product is clear. Keep service-level clocks running while a case awaits review. Monitor the destination-specific upper bound, coverage, transfers and actual delays weekly; keep an immediate rollback to the human queue. Use local human-adjudicated outcomes before activating any destination. See docs/sources.md and docs/evaluation-design.md.
