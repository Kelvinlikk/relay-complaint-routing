# Relay: two-minute stakeholder pitch

Spoken transcript of the narrated, captioned project video. The two demonstration complaints are fictional. The July test was retrospective: no complaints were transferred.

**00:00 · The intake decision**

A customer writes, “My payment never posted.” Is that a card payment or a mortgage payment?

A wrong transfer makes the customer wait while a second team reads the complaint.

Relay recommends a desk when the evidence is strong. Otherwise, staff decide.

**00:15 · What the model knows**

We trained on public Consumer Financial Protection Bureau complaints.

Relay reads the message, client mailbox and state when known. Adding a transformer did not improve the simpler model.

Only cards and mortgages passed our June checks.

**00:31 · Demo: card dispute**

Try two fictional complaints.

A customer disputes a duplicate credit card charge. Relay scores the card desk at 99.2%.

The desk passed its audit, so Relay recommends a handoff.

**00:46 · Demo: collections**

Now a collector keeps calling about a debt already paid.

Collections scores even higher, at 99.9%, but failed its audit.

A person still chooses the destination.

**00:59 · July: the later test**

We locked the rules in June, then tested them on 8,830 July complaints.

Relay recommended one desk for 928. Seventeen recommendations disagreed with the recorded product: 1.8% of recommendations, versus 12.5% with the historical rule.

No customer was transferred.

**01:22 · When staff keep the case**

For cases kept by staff, including short messages, we tested a two-desk shortlist.

In July it included the recorded product 91% of the time, versus 63% with keywords.

Does it save time? That needs a live test.

**01:39 · The pilot decision**

We recommend a pilot where staff confirm every handoff.

Audit 400 proposals per desk and randomly show or hide the shortlist to measure time and accuracy.

Under our cost assumptions, the July results imply $1,410 per 10,000 eligible complaints. The pilot should test that estimate.
