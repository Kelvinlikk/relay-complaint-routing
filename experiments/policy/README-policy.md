# Can a product queue earn permission to auto-route?

**Operational question.** A pooled error limit can hide a failing destination. We keep a frozen historical probability blend, adjust it for the 2026 mix, and give each queue one chance per audit date to demonstrate an error rate below 5%. The two successful destinations propose where to run a human-supervised trial; this is not live deployment or a guarantee.

## Protocol and independence
- Prepare the 16,789 published June 2026 narratives with at least ten whitespace-separated words. Remove exacts and near copies against the earlier historical sample and earlier June items using the baseline's normalized word-trigram MinHash/verified Jaccard procedure. This retains 14,642, including but not additive to the earlier June sample of 3,621. The candidate holdout July is screened against historical and June records by the external module.
- **June 1–7 (2,590 cases):** Fit only a global temperature and centered class offsets on log probabilities of the frozen historical TF-IDF + CatBoost blend (penalized multinomial likelihood, fixed regularization 0.002). Inputs remain the narrative plus independently known institution and optional state. This small correction adapts the queue mix without retraining the encoder. It does not establish that changing class priors were the only source of drift.
- **June 8–11 (3,127):** Freeze one cutoff per destination, from 0.50 to 0.98 in 0.02 increments. Choose maximum estimated coverage subject to at least 30 candidates and the one-sided 80% Wilson error limit at or below 4%. This is a heuristic for selecting candidates, not a safety certificate. Disable a queue if there is no candidate. The pooled benchmark uses the same procedure with one shared cutoff.
- **June 12–15 (1,552):** Check the fixed rules on new cases. For each queue test the null risk ≥5% by the exact one-sided binomial tail at 5% error, applying Holm's step-down correction across all six destinations. No queue passed. The June stress test and initial audit had already been seen in development. After that first no-go, we added a second month-end audit of the unchanged rules (no tuning on these observations). We report both checks and use 2.5% family-wise alpha per look across those two opportunities. These are working statistical criteria, not a prospective conditional guarantee: this extension was prompted by seeing June outcomes, and complaints can cluster.
- **June 12–30 cumulative safety audit (8,925):** Cards (cutoff 0.94; 6/444 errors; Holm p=0.000224) and mortgages (cutoff 0.86; 6/401; Holm p=0.000947) passed. Accounts and collections did not; credit reports and transfers had no candidate. Other always refers to review. We also froze a pooled benchmark at 0.94 (81/2,217 on this audit). Its success does not imply every destination meets the target.
- The June 16–30 replay uses the *midmonth policy* for the simulation and cannot be called an independent test of the *month-end policy*. Before examining any July labels we finalized the policy file with SHA-256 `2dc5b756de0255a9b2fd863d9981be2902b0064bc81868076a9a57b6822f0aa8`. June/July refer to receipt dates; archive publication and label availability can lag. The resulting July experiment is an untouched temporal holdout in a retrospective replay, not evidence about a live 2026 pilot.

## What happened in July
On 8,830 retained cases, the legacy policy routed 2,695 and misrouted 337. The unchanged 0.90 cutoff on recalibrated scores routed 3,104, misrouting 191 (6.15%). The audited pooled 0.94 cutoff routed 2,286; 112 were wrong (4.90%; 95% Wilson interval 4.09–5.86%). Within that acceptable-looking mean, 37/106 routed to credit reports and 9/64 routed to transfers were wrong. The destination-specific policy routed 928, of which 17 were wrong (1.83%, 95% Wilson interval 1.15–2.91%); the remaining 7,902 were human-review cases. Cards: 12/486; mortgages: 5/442. A temporal cluster check resampling the 28 represented receipt dates gives a 95% interval of 1.07–2.67% for gated misrouting. Counts use auto-routes as the error denominator; “all manual” has no measurable auto-route error rate.

At four assumed review minutes, $30/hour loaded wage and $25 incremental cost per misroute, the destination gate's scenario is +$1,621 per 10,000 **eligible** cases, versus +$2,007 for the less safe pooled rule and −$3,437 for the legacy policy. No staff time study, observed cash savings, production operational delays, fixed costs, audit costs or human disagreement are measured. If acquiring a new label took one complete four-minute review at the assumed wage, the entire 14,642-case calibration/tuning/audit sample would cost $29,284; at the illustrative $1,621 per 10,000 operating gain the gross-volume break-even is about 181,000 eligible cases before other overhead. This is an investment scenario, not a cost incurred in the archive analysis; incremental costs could be much smaller if labels already exist.

## Smoke check and reproduction
In the public edition, follow the [isolated source refit](../../docs/reproduce.md). In the private checkpoint edition, `bash reproduce.sh` fetches the archives, processes the dates in order, freezes the gates and evaluates July. The policy has no external model or network dependencies once the input probabilities exist. The reference run used fixed seeds and pinned libraries; small numerical differences on a different platform can alter concrete serialized floating-point values. All public JSON/CSV outputs are aggregate only. `apply(p_legacy, policy)` is the serving API; it returns adapted probabilities, suggested product, and whether the case qualifies under the tested score rule. Input/urgency/tenant guards are the caller's responsibility.
