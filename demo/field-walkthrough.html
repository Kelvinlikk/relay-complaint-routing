Two choices for a reviewer

For the roughly nine in ten complaints kept with people, ask a different question: was the recorded product on the list? The reviewer can always choose another desk.

Test it with people: Randomly compare two suggestions with the ordinary review screen, leaving “another desk” available. Measure both active seconds and adjudicated accuracy. A shortlist hit is neither of those outcomes.

Interpretation: A low error rate among routed cases comes at the cost of sending almost nine in ten cases to people. July validates a historical replay, not a live process.

Proposed action · not tested as a live service

Approve a shadow pilot.

Cards and Mortgages are the two candidate queues. The reviewer still decides every case; no transfers or customer resolutions are automated.

What a local rollout must establish

  • Before scoringVerify the receiving institution independently. Escalate urgent, unsupported-language or out-of-scope cases. The model does not detect these conditions.
  • Short storiesIn July, 5 of 29 proposed handoffs for messages under 50 words disagreed with the recorded product. This prompted a possible hold for short messages. In expanded May, it would have sent 43 more cases to review, including 2 mismatches.
  • Get local labelsUse one fixed look at 400 proposed local handoffs per desk. With 11 or fewer adjudicated errors, a desk passes; 12 or more fails. Independently adjudicate cases and check for clustered errors.
  • Measure valueIf the original June rule were eventually automated, the July base scenario values staff capacity at a net $1,410 per 10,000 eligible arrivals, assuming 4 minutes per review at $30/hour, $25 per misroute, and a 10% QA audit. Time real work and transfers before claiming savings.
